# Inharmonicity

--- <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inharmonicity>

--- <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inharmonicity#Inharmonicity_leads_to_stretched_tuning> and <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano_tuning#Stretch>

--- <https://youtu.be/tCsl6ZcY9ag?t=970>

**definition** _inharmonicity_ is the degree to which a sound's [[overtone]]s depart from perfect [[harmonic]]s

stiff strings under low tension, such as the bass strings of an upright piano, show greater [[inharmonicity]], while flexible strings under high tension, such as the treble strings of a piano, show lesser [[inharmonicity]]. wound strings and longer strings tend to have lesser [[inharmonicity]] than equivalent solid strings and equivalent shorter strings

[[inharmonicity]] is especially notable in pianos, varying not only from instrument to instrument but also from register to register within an instrument. an internally harmonious piano tune corrects for the instrument's [[inharmonicity]], and in general bass registers end up flat while treble registers end up sharp. tuning a piano electronically always begins with a measurement of [[inharmonicity]] across its registers
