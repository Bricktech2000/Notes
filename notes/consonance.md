# Consonance

**see** [[inharmonicity]]

> **resource** _The Physics Of Dissonance_ by minutephysics, and why [[consonance]] comes from [[overtone]]s --- <https://youtu.be/tCsl6ZcY9ag>

musical [[consonance]] comes from [[overtone]]s lining up, not from the [[frequency]] ratios of [[musical interval]]s. pure sine waves---if they're at least a minor third $\frac 6 5$ or so apart --- <https://youtu.be/tCsl6ZcY9ag?t=258>---will never sound dissonant because pure sine waves don't have any [[overtone]]s to begin with

when the [[overtone]]s are [[harmonic]]s, which is the case with strings and pipes (and the human voice), then, indeed, [[musical interval]]s consisting of low-order ratios are more consonant than those consisting of high-order ratios. but when the [[overtone]]s are not [[harmonic]]s, like with bars and bells, then those low-order ratios that are so important in western music sound dissonant
